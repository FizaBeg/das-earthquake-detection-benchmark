Distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) turns telecommunication fibres into dense seismic arrays, but its records are noisy and labelled examples are scarce. Several deep-learning architectures have been proposed for detecting earthquakes in DAS data, yet they are rarely compared under the same conditions. We compare four families of spatiotemporal models: a two-dimensional convolutional neural network (CNN), a CNN followed by a long short-term memory network (CNN--LSTM), a convolutional LSTM (ConvLSTM) and a patch-based Transformer. We use a public, labelled dataset of real DAS recordings made from 2016 to 2019 on telecommunication fibres under the Stanford University campus, with %%NTRAIN%% training, %%NEVAL%% evaluation and %%NTEST%% test examples of earthquakes and noise. All models receive the same reduced input (48 channels by 320 samples), have about 46,000 to 150,000 parameters, and are trained with the same budget on a CPU with five random seeds. Statistical significance is assessed with Friedman, paired bootstrap and McNemar tests. With all labels, mean test ROC-AUC ranged from %%AUCMIN%% (Transformer) to %%AUCMAX%% (CNN--LSTM), but no pairwise difference between seed ensembles was significant, and accuracy at a 0.5 threshold was unstable between seeds (for the CNN it ranged from %%ACCMIN_CNN%% to %%ACCMAX_CNN%%). When synthetic white noise was added to the real test records at a signal-to-noise ratio of $-5$\,dB, the mean ROC-AUC loss was %%N5DROP_CNN%% for the CNN, %%N5DROP_CNNLSTM%% for the CNN--LSTM, %%N5DROP_CONVLSTM%% for the ConvLSTM and %%N5DROP_TRANS%% for the Transformer, and the Transformer ensemble was significantly better than the CNN and CNN--LSTM ensembles. Setting up to half of the channels to zero reduced ROC-AUC by at most %%D50MAX%% for all models. With only 1\,\% of the training labels (%%N001%% examples) the ranking reversed: the CNN--LSTM and CNN kept ROC-AUC of about %%F001LO%%--%%F001HI%%, whereas the Transformer fell to %%F001TRANSM%%. All models processed a 25.6-s window in at most about 13\,ms on one CPU thread. On this array, architecture choice matters little for clean data but strongly affects robustness to noise and label efficiency, so DAS detectors should be compared over several seeds and under perturbed conditions, not only by single-run accuracy.
